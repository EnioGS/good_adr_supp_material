### What typically makes your team write a decision record? (multiple answers)

- 33 (66%) The decision is expensive or hard to reverse

### Does your team use a template for decision records? (one answer)

- 7 (14%) No template, but a shared informal habit
- 4 (8%) (no answer)
- 2 (4%) No, everyone writes them differently

### Of the elements above, which three would you keep if a record could contain nothing else? (multiple answers)

- 35 (70%) The reasoning that led to the choice (the "why")

- 15 (30%) The trade-offs knowingly accepted

- 6 (12%) The decision's current status (proposed / accepted / superseded)

- 2 (4%) The quality attributes or use cases the decision serves
- 2 (4%) Diagrams where they aid understanding
- 1 (2%) Links or references to the implementation

### What is the longest a decision record should normally be? (one answer)

### Which statement is closest to your view on templates? (one answer)

- 36 (72%) A template is useful as a reminder, but should be adapted freely
- 13 (26%) A strict template should be followed, so records are consistent
- 1 (2%) Experienced writers don't need a template; the structure can be implicit

### Which decisions deserve a record? (one answer)

- 25 (50%) It depends entirely on project size and context
- 8 (16%) Any decision that affects more than one team or component
- 8 (16%) Any decision where the choice was not obvious
- 7 (14%) Only decisions that are hard or expensive to reverse
- 2 (4%) All architectural decisions, regardless of size

- 7 (14%) Other (written in by the respondent)

### When a decision changes, what should happen to the original record? (one answer)

- 21 (42%) Leave it unchanged; write a new record that supersedes it

### Which matters more for decision records? (one answer)

- 14 (28%) Neither — what matters is that they're searchable, wherever they live
- 12 (24%) Both matter equally
- 1 (2%) Keeping them in a shared wiki, so non-developers can find them
