# Interview Guide – Perceptions of High‑Quality Architecture Decision Records (ADRs)

---

## 1 Opening & Ethics

*Brief script for interviewer*

* Welcome & thank the participant.
* State the study goal: *“We want to understand how experienced software architects perceive a ‘good’ ADR, the hurdles they face, and the impact ADRs have on their projects.”*
* Confirm consent to record, assure anonymity, and explain how the data will be used and stored.

---

## 2 Background & Context

Open‑ended prompts – adapt follow‑ups as needed.

1. **Current role** – Could you tell me about your responsibilities as a software architect today?
2. **Experience timeline** – About how many years have you developed software, and how many of those have focused on architecture?
3. **Team size** – Roughly how many people contribute code on your current project?
4. **Organisation type** – How would you describe the organisation you work in (startup, mid‑size product company, large enterprise, consultancy, government agency, other)?
5. **Team distribution** – Is your team collocated, distributed within one country, or globally distributed? How does that shape collaboration?
6. **ADR frequency** – How often do ADRs get produced in your project? Feel free to outline typical triggers.
7. **Formal training** – Have you had formal courses or certifications in software architecture? If so, which?
8. **ADR‑specific training** – Have you taken any workshops or courses specifically on ADRs or decision documentation?
9. **Project domain & scale** – What kinds of systems do you usually work on (e.g., web apps, embedded, data/ML)? Roughly how large are they (small <10 KLOC, medium 10–100 KLOC, large >100 KLOC, or another measure you prefer)?

---

## 3 Understanding & Perceived Purpose of ADRs

* In your own words, **what is an ADR?**
* **Why** do you create ADRs? What primary needs do they address?
* Compared with other documentation, **what unique value** do ADRs bring?

---

## 4 Characteristics of a High‑Quality ADR

* When you read an ADR, **what must be present** for you to trust and reuse it?
* How does **structure or formatting** influence its usefulness?
* Could you recall an ADR you thought was **particularly well‑written**? What made it stand out?
* Conversely, what issues have you seen in ADRs that **undermine** their usefulness?

---

## 5 Creation, Maintenance & Tooling

* Walk me through **how a new ADR is produced** in your team.
* **What obstacles** arise while writing *or keeping* ADRs up to date? (Organisational, cultural, technical, time‑related…)
* How do these challenges **affect whether ADRs are consulted** later on?
* **Strategies** – What have you found helps overcome these challenges?
* **Tools & templates** – Do you use any tools, templates, or automation to create or maintain ADRs? How do they help or hinder?

---

## 6 Impact on Project Outcomes

* Can you share **examples where ADRs improved collaboration** or decision‑making?
* Have you seen ADRs **influence code quality, maintainability, or defect rates**? How so?
* Overall, **how critical** would you say ADRs are to project success?

---

## 7 Good Practices & Opportunities for Improvement

* Which **practices** consistently lead to better ADRs in your experience?
* Looking at your current approach or templates, **what would you most like to improve**?
* Are there **innovations**—tools, processes, or formats—you’re excited to try?

---

## 8 Reflections on Published Guidance *(optional / skippable)*

*Feel free to skip if not applicable.*

* Are you aware of any **published guidelines or articles**—academic or industry—about ADRs?
* If so, **how does your practice align or differ** from those guidelines? Any gaps you notice?

---

## 9 Future Directions

* How do you **see ADR practice evolving** in your projects over the next few years?
* Are there **emerging tools or methodologies** you believe could transform how architects document decisions?

---

## 10 Closing

* Is there **anything else** you’d like to add about ADRs that we haven’t covered?
* Thank the participant for their time and insight.

---

### Data Collection & Analysis (for researcher reference)

* Interviews recorded and transcribed verbatim.
* Transcripts coded using open and axial coding to derive themes.
* Themes will inform the design of subsequent survey instruments and validation steps.
